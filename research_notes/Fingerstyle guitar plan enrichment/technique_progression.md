# Technique Progression for a 12-Month Adult-Beginner Fingerstyle Plan (toward Oshio / Sungha Jung)

Scope note: plan content was read from `app/curriculum.json` (monthlyPlans, exercises, fingering). Current technique content in brief: Pattern A (p-i-m-a quarters, 40–50 BPM), Pattern B (p-i-m-a eighths, 45–55 BPM test), alternating thumb from Month 4 (quarter-note bass, 40–50 BPM learning, 50–60 BPM optional test), melody-balance from Month 5, one hammer-on/pull-off/slide in Month 7, an optional 12th-fret natural harmonic in Month 9, and no percussion, barre work (full F "optional later"), palm muting, pinch drill, rest stroke, monotonic bass or alternate tuning anywhere. The Month 10 dream-piece note says "Skip percussion" and "Keep standard tuning as default".

Research constraint: about 20 tool calls. Several primary pages (JustinGuitar, Guitar World Tommy Emmanuel masterclass, gtdb.org tuning database, NamuWiki Oshio page) returned 403 or paywalls, so some claims rely on secondary teaching sites. Method books (Hal Leonard Fingerstyle Guitar Method, Mark Hanson, Grossman) were not accessible online; nothing below is quoted from them.

## 1. Core technique sequence (PIMA, strokes, thumb independence, Travis, pinches, arpeggios, melody-over-bass, slurs, vibrato)

### Takeaway
Teaching sources converge on: PIMA assignment with free stroke → isolated thumb → steady alternating bass alone → add treble notes one at a time → pinches → arpeggio variants → melody over bass → slurs (hammer-ons already appear inside Travis lessons) → rest-stroke melody emphasis later. The current plan follows this order roughly but delays the thumb-alone bass work until Month 4, has no pinch or monotonic-bass drill, and puts hammer-ons at Month 7.

### Cited Findings
- A beginner fingerstyle framework orders learning as: posture → p-i-m-a string assignment → stroke types (free vs rest) → single-finger repetition → basic alternation → thumb independence → pinch → full ascending arpeggio → fretting-hand refinement → pattern variations. — [Guitar Wiz: Fingerstyle foundation](https://guitarwiz.app/articles/fingerstyle-guitar-foundation/)
- Same source: start with free stroke for everything; add rest stroke for melody emphasis only after basic coordination is solid. Rest stroke gives "louder, fuller tone"; free stroke is standard for arpeggios. — [Guitar Wiz](https://guitarwiz.app/articles/fingerstyle-guitar-foundation/)
- Classical definitions: rest stroke (apoyando) lands on the adjacent string; free stroke (tirando) does not; free stroke is the more fundamental and more frequently used stroke. — [Wikipedia: Classical guitar technique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_guitar_technique); Douglas Niedt teaches rest stroke specifically to bring a melody out of an arpeggio — [Niedt: Rest stroke for melody within an arpeggio](https://douglasniedt.com/reststrokeformelodyofarpeggiopart1.html)
- Jamie Stillway's Acoustic Guitar Travis-picking lesson sequence: (1) master alternating bass with the thumb alone (root–fifth, root–third, combined), (2) add treble notes as quarters, then eighths, then syncopations with i and m, (3) apply to a progression (Dm–G–C), (4) add melodic ideas including hammer-ons, (5) arrange simple melodies. Also mentions palm-muting the bass. — [Acoustic Guitar: Nuts and bolts of Travis picking](https://acousticguitar.com/lesson-learn-the-nuts-and-bolts-of-travis-picking/)
- Travis bass-string patterns are defined by string order: 6-4-6-4, 6-4-5-4, 5-4-5-4, 5-4-6-4 depending on chord root; bass alternates root and fifth. — [Fender: Travis picking](https://www.fender.com/articles/techniques/travis-picking-on-guitar); [Guitar Wiz: Travis picking](https://guitarwiz.app/articles/travis-picking-pattern/)
- Practise the bass alone "over and over"; count "1 and 2 and" aloud so voice and thumb stay in sync while fingers add notes between. — summarised from Travis lessons in [Tomas Michaud](https://tomasmichaud.com/travis-fingerpicking/) / [Lauren Bateman](https://www.laurenbateman.com/how-to-travis-pick-on-guitar/) (search-snippet level; not individually fetched)
- Adam Rafferty's independence drills: thumb should sit out past the fingers and move from its large joint; Drill 1 play thumb without fingers moving; Drill 2 fingers without thumb moving; Drill 3 sing the groove, play thumb pattern alone, then combine while keeping mental focus on the bassline. — [Adam Rafferty: Thumb and finger independence](https://www.adamrafferty.com/2015/01/03/thumb-and-finger-independence-for-fingerstyle-guitarists/)
- Tommy Emmanuel: first steps to independence are "the fingers play this, and the thumb plays a solid time"; work complex movements slowly with a metronome before bringing them up to tempo. — [Guitar World: Tommy Emmanuel's guide to hand independence](https://www.guitarworld.com/lessons/tommy-emmanuels-guide-developing-hand-independence-and-more-el-vaquero-video) (via search summary; masterclass page itself was paywalled) ; [MusicRadar: Tommy Emmanuel's 8 tips](https://www.musicradar.com/news/guitars/tommy-emmanuels-8-tips-for-guitarists-632783)
- TrueFire has a Tommy Emmanuel "Fingerstyle Milestones" course and "Enhancing Your Thumb Speed" exercises (playalong), confirming thumb-speed work is a named stage in his teaching. — [TrueFire: Enhancing your thumb speed](https://truefire.com/tommy-emmanuel-guitar-lessons/fingestyle-breakthroughs/enhancing-your-thumb-speed-exercise-2-playalong/v34221); [JamPlay/TrueFire: Fingerstyle Milestones](https://jamplay.com/guitar-lessons/fingerstyle-milestones)
- Sungha Jung's music uses legato (hammer-ons/pull-offs), barre chords and glissando (slides) in "most songs". — [PlayFingerstyleGuitar: How to play like Sungha Jung](https://www.playfingerstyleguitar.com/how-to-play-like-sungha-jung)

### Inferences
- Move thumb-alone alternating bass from Month 4 to a 5-minute daily strand starting Month 2 (after Pattern A is steady), so that by Month 4 the learner is adding treble notes rather than starting the thumb from zero. Both Stillway and Rafferty treat thumb-alone as the foundation, not a later add-on.
- Add a **monotonic (steady root) bass** drill in Month 2–3: repeated eighth- or quarter-note roots under a melody. Most Jung/Oshio pop arrangements lean on a steady root bass more than on Travis alternation, and it is the easier independence step (inferred; no source quantified this).
- Add an explicit **pinch** drill (p + a together on bass + string 1) in Month 1–2; the plan's layers exercise asks for simultaneous bass+melody but never isolates it.
- Pull hammer-ons/pull-offs/slides forward to Month 4–5 as short technique strands (Stillway places hammer-ons inside the Travis sequence), keeping Month 7 as the "ornament in repertoire" month.
- Introduce rest stroke with **a** or **m** for melody emphasis in Month 5 (melody balance month); it is the most direct mechanical tool for the "melody buried" fault.
- Vibrato: add in Month 7 alongside ornaments (small, in-time wrist/finger vibrato on sustained melody notes).

- No accessible primary source gave a month-by-month schedule; Hal Leonard Fingerstyle Guitar Method and Mark Hanson's "Art of Contemporary Travis Picking" contents could not be retrieved, so their exact ordering is unverified.
- No sourced guidance on vibrato timing for fingerstyle beginners.

## 2. Signature modern-percussive techniques and what is realistic in year one

### Takeaway
The single most transferable Oshio/Jung technique for a year-one learner is the backbeat "snare" (thumb slap or finger slap on beats 2 and 4) — teachers call it beginner-friendly. Natural harmonics are also year-one material; harp/artificial harmonics, Oshio's nail attack, tapping and multiple alternate tunings are year-two material. Drop D and one open/DADGAD piece are a reasonable late-year experiment.

### Cited Findings
- Oshio's technique includes fingerpicking, tap harmonics and a "nail attack" strum in which he slaps the strings with the nails of the middle and ring fingers. — [Wikipedia: Kotaro Oshio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotaro_Oshio)
- The nail attack was his own invention to keep a percussive beat going while plucking a melody at the same time. — [Acoustic Accent blog](https://acousticaccent.wordpress.com/2015/11/05/kotaro-oshio-fingerstyle-perfection-and-nail-attack/)
- Oshio frequently uses DADGAD and also CGDGBD (a Michael Hedges tuning); "Twilight" tabs are in standard tuning; "Fight!" is played half-step down. — search summary of [Ultimate Guitar: Twilight](https://tabs.ultimate-guitar.com/tab/kotaro-oshio/twilight-tabs-518475) and related tab pages (secondary; gtdb.org tuning list returned 403 — treat tuning attributions as needing score-level confirmation)
- Sungha Jung's basic percussion in "almost all" songs is a thumb slap on beats 2 and 4 using a swinging wrist motion; he also favours slapped harmonics on a node; other techniques listed: legato, barre chords, glissando, natural harmonics, tapping, alternate tunings, rasgueado, palm muting. — [PlayFingerstyleGuitar: How to play like Sungha Jung](https://www.playfingerstyleguitar.com/how-to-play-like-sungha-jung)
- Same source recommends starting with his simpler originals "Siesta", "L'Atelier", then "Tadhana"; avoid "Racing Into The Night" until fundamentals are solid; Jung uses acrylic-strengthened nails (an idea from Oshio), no fingerpicks, occasional thumbpick. — [PlayFingerstyleGuitar](https://www.playfingerstyleguitar.com/how-to-play-like-sungha-jung)
- Jung tunings: "The Milky Way" in DADGAD; "Felicity" in an open tuning; "On Cloud Nine" in an open low-E major tuning (half step below Felicity's). — search summary of [PlayFingerstyleGuitar: Top Sungha Jung songs](https://www.playfingerstyleguitar.com/songs/top-sungha-jung) (secondary)
- Doug Young's percussive lesson order: (1) backbeat slap on strings 3–5 on beats 2 and 4 with a metronome/drum machine, (2) add fingerstyle chords with the slaps, (3) syncopation over the backbeat, (4) syncopated progressions, (5) sustaining notes around the slap, (6) fingernail-back accents, (7) backbeat on only 2 or 4, (8) a full piece. — [Acoustic Guitar: Percussive fingerstyle techniques (Doug Young)](https://acousticguitar.com/learn-these-percussive-fingerstyle-guitar-techniques-to-add-punch-and-groove-to-your-playing/)
- Thumb slap is described as "one of the most popular, intuitive" techniques "for beginners and advanced"; strike with the side of the thumb on the string of the bass note so it snaps against the fretboard, via a quick wrist "whip". — [BlitzGuitar: Thumb slap](https://www.blitzguitar.com/five-fingerpicking-tips-save-months-practice/thumb-slap-and-thumb-slap-strum-percussive-technique-on-acoustic-guitar/); [BlitzGuitar: most simple percussive technique](https://www.blitzguitar.com/the-most-simple-percussive-guitar-technique-for-beginners/)
- Harp (cascading) harmonics: fret a note, touch 12 frets higher with the picking-hand index, pluck with the thumb, alternating with normally plucked notes from the ring finger. — [PlayFingerstyleGuitar: Guide to harmonics](https://www.playfingerstyleguitar.com/advanced/guide-to-playing-harmonics); [Wikipedia: String harmonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_harmonic)
- Jeff Gunn's artificial-harmonic sequence: single chimed note (fret 2 string 6, touch 12 frets up, pluck with thumb) → harmonics around a held chord shape → harmonics plus three-note chord accents → full barred-chord harmonics → moving between 12- and 7-fret offsets; "beginning guitarists should master basic fretting and chord shapes before attempting these techniques." — [Acoustic Guitar: 5 ways to master artificial harmonics](https://acousticguitar.com/lesson-5-ways-to-master-artificial-harmonics-on-an-acoustic-guitar/)
- Tommy Emmanuel's toolkit taught in lessons includes Travis picking and harp harmonics. — [Guitar Player: Tommy Emmanuel lesson](https://www.guitarplayer.com/lessons/tommy-emmanuel-shred-lesson)
- DADGAD is an open Dsus4 tuning. — [Wikipedia: DADGAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DADGAD)

### Inferences (proposed year-one order)
1. **Months 2–3: palm-muted bass** (side of palm near the saddle) under Pattern A/B — cheap, and it shapes the "drum" feel of modern arrangements (Stillway mentions palm-muting bass).
2. **Month 4–6: backbeat snare** (Doug Young step 1: slap strings 3–5 on 2 and 4 over a held chord; then thumb-slap variant replacing the bass on 2 and 4). Start at a tempo where the ungrooved version is easy; one 5-minute strand. This directly contradicts the Month 10 "skip percussion" advice — percussion should be introduced earlier and small, not avoided.
3. **Month 6–7: natural harmonics** at frets 12, 7, 5 (currently Month 9, optional) — they are a "touch-don't-press" skill with no prerequisites beyond pick-hand control.
4. **Month 8–9: finger strum / down-up index strum and simple rasgueado-like flick** combined with the backbeat (the precursor to Oshio's nail attack).
5. **Month 9–10: drop D** (one-string change, low risk) for the dream excerpt; **DADGAD or one Jung/Oshio open tuning** only for the specific 4–8 bar excerpt.
6. **Month 10–12 optional: single harp-harmonic chime** (Gunn step 1) and slapped harmonics at fret 12.
7. **Year two:** full harp-harmonic cascades, Oshio's nail attack at tempo, two-hand tapping, CGDGBD and frequent tuning changes, body percussion layered with independent melody.

## 3. Benchmarks, tempos, note rates, and common faults with fixes

### Takeaway
Published beginner lessons almost never give BPM targets; the one explicit figure found is 50–60 BPM for foundation patterns where the pattern "feels almost too easy". The benchmarks below are therefore inferences, anchored to that figure and to the plan's own 40–60 BPM numbers.

### Cited Findings
- Foundation practice at 50–60 BPM, where the pattern feels "almost too easy"; expect a week minimum for single-finger evenness; thumb independence develops over "weeks to months". — [Guitar Wiz](https://guitarwiz.app/articles/fingerstyle-guitar-foundation/)
- Stillway's Travis lesson and Doug Young's percussion lesson give no BPM; both say take examples slowly and build a consistent backbeat/rhythm before moving on. — [Acoustic Guitar: Travis](https://acousticguitar.com/lesson-learn-the-nuts-and-bolts-of-travis-picking/); [Acoustic Guitar: Percussive](https://acousticguitar.com/learn-these-percussive-fingerstyle-guitar-techniques-to-add-punch-and-groove-to-your-playing/)
- Tommy Emmanuel-style rule: play something as slow as needed to get it with good time and no mistakes at least twice, then raise tempo. — [Riffhard: How to play like Tommy Emmanuel](https://www.riffhard.com/how-to-play-guitar-like-tommy-emmanuel/) (search summary)
- Faults and fixes table: scratchy tone from over-curled fingers → relaxed curl, fingertip pad; weak thumb → isolate thumb in separate exercises; muddy sound → passive muting; premature complexity → master one variation first; tension/locked wrist → float the hand. — [Guitar Wiz](https://guitarwiz.app/articles/fingerstyle-guitar-foundation/)
- Thumb drifting with fingers → slow down and isolate the thumb until automatic; uneven volume → record yourself and balance bass and treble; learn bass line, melody separately before combining. — [AcousticLife: Top fingerstyle mistakes](https://acousticlife.tv/top-5-fingerstyle-guitar-mistakes/) (search summary)
- If the bass becomes shaky "the whole structure will quickly collapse"; keep the bassline rock solid against melody. — [Guitar World: fingerstyle blues with alternating bass](https://www.guitarworld.com/lessons/how-to-play-fingerstyle-blues-with-alternating-bass-and-melody) (search summary)
- Thumb should sit out past the fingers and not hide behind them; move from its base joint. — [Adam Rafferty](https://www.adamrafferty.com/2015/01/03/thumb-and-finger-independence-for-fingerstyle-guitarists/)
- Rest stroke on the melody note to bring it out of an arpeggio. — [Douglas Niedt](https://douglasniedt.com/reststrokeformelodyofarpeggiopart1.html)

### Inferences (proposed benchmarks; note rate = notes per second)
| By end of month | Benchmark | Tempo / note rate |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pattern A over C–Am–Em–G, 16 bars; pinch p+a on each chord | 50–60 BPM quarters = 0.8–1.0 n/s |
| 2 | Pattern B eighths through changes; thumb-alone alternating bass on Am/C/Em, 8 bars | 55–65 BPM eighths = 1.8–2.2 n/s; bass 60 BPM quarters |
| 3 | Monotonic eighth-note root bass under a 4-bar melody; palm-muted bass option | 60 BPM eighths (2 n/s) |
| 4 | Travis p-i-p-m (fingers on the &) through C–Am–Em–G, plus one pinch per bar | 60–70 BPM, eighths (2.0–2.3 n/s) |
| 5 | Same at 70–80 BPM; rest-stroke melody note audibly louder than bass on recording; hammer-on/pull-off in time | ~2.3–2.7 n/s |
| 6 | Backbeat slap on 2 and 4 over a held chord, 16 bars without drifting; natural harmonics at 12/7/5 ring clearly on strings 1–6 | 70–80 BPM |
| 7–8 | Thumb slap replaces bass on 2 & 4 under a simple melody; vibrato on long notes; first partial barre in a piece | 70–90 BPM |
| 9–10 | Drop-D or DADGAD 4–8 bar excerpt; finger strum + backbeat groove | piece tempo, often 70–90% of recording |
| 12 | Three-piece set; Travis at 80–90 BPM eighths (≈2.7–3.0 n/s); one percussive piece | — |
- These are deliberately modest: many Jung/Oshio pieces run well above 100 BPM with sixteenth-note textures; year-one excerpts should be played at a personal tempo, not the recording tempo (inference).
- Fault fixes to add to the plan's troubleshooting: (a) thumb collapses into the fingers or speeds up on easy notes → thumb-alone 8 bars, then add one treble note per bar, count aloud (Stillway/Rafferty); (b) melody buried → play melody alone at target volume, then add bass with lighter thumb, then use rest stroke on melody notes (Niedt); (c) tension → float the wrist, check for squeezing the neck, use minimum fretting pressure, stop at any pain (Guitar Wiz).

## 4. Left-hand development: barres, partial barres, shifts, safe stretching

### Takeaway
Structured beginner courses place barre chords at the end of the beginner stage (JustinGuitar: Module 22, end of Grade 3), and Sungha Jung's repertoire uses barre chords routinely, so a plan aimed at his style should schedule partial barres from mid-year and a full F/Bm by months 8–10 rather than leaving full F "optional" all year.

### Cited Findings
- JustinGuitar introduces barre chords in Module 22, the final module of Grade 3 ("Advancing Beginner"), after consolidating the beginner material; the same grade contains a fingerstyle module. — [JustinGuitar: Beginner consolidation & easy barre chords](https://www.justinguitar.com/modules/beginner-consolidation-easy-barre-chords); [JustinGuitar Beginner Course](https://www.justinguitar.com/beginner) (via search summary; the lesson pages returned 403)
- JustinGuitar has an "Easy Barre Chords for Beginners" lesson, i.e. partial/easier barres precede full barres. — [JustinGuitar: Easy barre chords](https://www.justinguitar.com/guitar-lessons/easy-barre-chords-for-beginners-bg-2303) (title only; content blocked)
- Sungha Jung songs commonly use barre chords, legato and glissando. — [PlayFingerstyleGuitar](https://www.playfingerstyleguitar.com/how-to-play-like-sungha-jung)
- Artificial/harp harmonics exercises assume a held barre chord shape (G barre in Gunn's step 2 and full-string barre in step 4). — [Acoustic Guitar: Jeff Gunn](https://acousticguitar.com/lesson-5-ways-to-master-artificial-harmonics-on-an-acoustic-guitar/)
- Tension fix: avoid a locked wrist; fret with the minimum needed pressure. — [Guitar Wiz](https://guitarwiz.app/articles/fingerstyle-guitar-foundation/)

### Inferences
- Month 3–4: G shape variants and Cadd9/G/D with anchored 3rd/4th fingers (common in Jung covers); position shift drills sliding a fixed shape along one string (links to slides).
- Month 5: small F already present; add a 2-string index barre (strings 1–2) in context, and Bm7 partial (x20202).
- Month 6–7: 3–4 string partial barres (e.g., F xx3211 → F with index across 4 strings), 1–2 minutes a day, stop at pain.
- Month 8–10: full F (133211) and Bm (x24432) in a piece; E-shape barre shift between two frets. Months 10–12 keep barre work short and musical.
- Safe stretching: warm up with easy material; stretch shapes starting higher up the neck (smaller frets) and move down; limit stretch-heavy work to a few minutes; never practise through sharp pain (general guidance, not source-backed here).
- Given the learner already has small F, full F by ~Month 8–9 is realistic at 50 min/day.

### Gaps
- No medical/physiotherapy source on safe stretching for guitarists was retrieved.
- No source gave a month-level barre timeline specific to fingerstyle.

## 5. Specific drills from respected teachers, and where the current plan should change

### Takeaway
Concrete, attributable drills found: Stillway's thumb-first Travis sequence, Rafferty's three-step independence drill, Tommy Emmanuel's "thumb keeps solid time while fingers play" and slow-with-metronome rule, Doug Young's eight-step backbeat progression, and Jeff Gunn's five-step harmonic progression. The plan should (1) start thumb work earlier, (2) add pinch, monotonic bass, palm muting and rest stroke, (3) introduce backbeat percussion by mid-year instead of skipping it, (4) move harmonics earlier and make them non-optional, (5) schedule partial-to-full barres, and (6) add a drop-D/DADGAD step before the dream piece.

### Cited Findings
- Stillway (Acoustic Guitar) Travis sequence — bass alone, then treble quarters/eighths/syncopation, progression, hammer-ons, melody. — [Acoustic Guitar](https://acousticguitar.com/lesson-learn-the-nuts-and-bolts-of-travis-picking/)
- Rafferty independence drills 1–3. — [Adam Rafferty](https://www.adamrafferty.com/2015/01/03/thumb-and-finger-independence-for-fingerstyle-guitarists/)
- Emmanuel: thumb solid time against fingers; slow with metronome. — [Guitar World](https://www.guitarworld.com/lessons/tommy-emmanuels-guide-developing-hand-independence-and-more-el-vaquero-video); [TrueFire: 13 free Tommy Emmanuel lessons](https://blog.truefire.com/guitar-lessons/13-free-tommy-emmanuel-fingerstyle-guitar-lessons/)
- Doug Young backbeat progression. — [Acoustic Guitar](https://acousticguitar.com/learn-these-percussive-fingerstyle-guitar-techniques-to-add-punch-and-groove-to-your-playing/)
- Jeff Gunn harmonics progression. — [Acoustic Guitar](https://acousticguitar.com/lesson-5-ways-to-master-artificial-harmonics-on-an-acoustic-guitar/)
- Jung himself recommended the "AllEars method" to learners of his style; a "Sungha Jung Guitar Lesson" YouTube playlist exists. — [PlayFingerstyleGuitar](https://www.playfingerstyleguitar.com/how-to-play-like-sungha-jung) (claim via search snippet); [YouTube playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLGCOijrGJ1-Qyu5_SaD_Wn7Xn7N7Rg6Dl)
- Easier Jung originals for learners: Siesta, L'Atelier, Tadhana. — [PlayFingerstyleGuitar](https://www.playfingerstyleguitar.com/how-to-play-like-sungha-jung)
